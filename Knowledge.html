Norman's Law “When external pressure exceeds internal regulation, instability follows.” The foundational principle of The Norman System. Everything else is measurement, diagnosis, and response. CORE CONCEPTS Norman's Gap The structural distance between external pressure and internal regulation. When the Gap widens, instability follows. Law #2 of The Norman System. Gap Index / Gap Score The quantified measure of the Gap. Positive means strain. The wider it grows, the higher the organizational risk. Signal Compression The distortion of operational signals as they travel from the floor to leadership. Compressed signals produce decisions based on incomplete reality. Norman Decision Window (NDW) The available time between when a problem is recognized and when a decision must be made. Narrow windows produce poor decisions under pressure. Ownership Diffusion The dispersal of accountability across too many people or roles. When everyone owns something, no one does. A primary driver of escalation failure. KPI Truth The degree to which a KPI reflects operational reality rather than a managed or cosmetic version of it. Low KPI Truth accelerates Signal Compression. Signal Flow Index A composite measure of how well signals travel through the tiered meeting structure -- from floor to leadership without distortion or delay. Internal Regulation The organization's capacity to sense, decide, and act in response to external pressure. The right side of the Gap equation. IMI Maturity Framework Seven levels of MOS implementation maturity: Dark, Named, Started, Compliant, Regulating, Embedded, Compounding. North Star Metric: % of elements at Level 4+. THE SEVEN LAWS The Norman System is built on seven laws of organizational instability. Each law describes a predictable failure condition. 1 Norman's Law -- When external pressure exceeds internal regulation, instability follows. 2 The Gap Law -- The structural distance between pressure and regulation is measurable, predictable, and closeable. 3 The Signal Law -- Organizations fail not because problems exist, but because signals about those problems are compressed before they reach decision-makers. 4 The Ownership Law -- Diffused accountability produces no accountability. Every critical function requires a single named owner. 5 The Decision Window Law -- Every problem has a window in which it can be resolved without compounding. Miss the window and the cost multiplies. 6 The Regulation Law -- Internal regulation is not a byproduct of culture. It is a system. Systems can be built, measured, and improved. 7 The Compounding Law -- Organizations that regulate well under low pressure will compound that advantage under high pressure. The Gap works in both directions. BOOKS Four books. One operating system. Each book addresses a different layer of the system -- operational, physiological, human, and diagnostic.
