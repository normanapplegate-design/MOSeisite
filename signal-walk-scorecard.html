SignalWalk

Tier meeting observation scorecard

Score a walk first, then your result shows here.

Why this exists

This is a personal field tool, not a client deliverable. It scores a live tier meeting against the six dimensions that separate a meeting that regulates from one that just occupies a slot on the calendar. It produces a diagnosis on the spot, not just an impression you have to write up later.

Purpose

Sit in on any Tier 1, 2, or 3 meeting at a client site. Rate each dimension in real time using the three-button scale, drop a short evidence note, and walk out with a scored profile and an IMI read for that tier.

Inputs

Site name, date, tier observed, and a Good, Drifting, or Failure mode rating for each of the six dimensions below. The evidence note is optional but is what turns the score into client-ready language.

Outputs

A profile bar chart across the six dimensions, an overall IMI maturity band for that meeting, and a saved history entry so repeat visits to the same site show drift or improvement.

Cadence

Use it every time you observe a tier meeting in the field. Score the same site again on a later visit to see whether the meeting is regulating better or worse than last time.

Escalation path

This tool does not escalate anything itself. A low score or a Failure mode rating on Escalation or Actions is the signal to raise the pattern directly with the chair or the client sponsor before the next scheduled walk.

Supporting documents

Pairs with the Tier Meeting Diagnostic and the Meeting Signal Review. This tool is the only one of the three meant for your own use in the room rather than for the client to fill out.

Three things most likely to fail in year one

Scoring from memory after the meeting instead of live, in the room. Skipping the evidence note because the meeting felt rushed, which erases the exact language you need later. Treating one weak visit as the verdict instead of checking it against the history trend.

The six dimensions

Start

Good: starts on time, every time, attendees pre-prepared.

What can go wrong: the meeting starts late waiting for one person, or the pre-read never got done, so the first several minutes are spent catching people up instead of deciding anything.

Focus

Good: driven by the board or the data. Numbers first, opinions second.

What can go wrong: driven by personalities instead of data. The loudest voice in the room sets the agenda regardless of what the board actually shows.

Energy

Good: short, sharp, standing, time-boxed by topic.

What can go wrong: long, seated, and sliding into problem-solving in the room instead of assigning it and moving on.

Actions

Good: every action has a named owner and a due date. Closed actions get checked for whether they actually worked.

What can go wrong: "we should look into that" gets said and the meeting moves on. No owner, no date, and it quietly disappears.

Escalation

Good: issues move up to the next tier at the defined threshold, before they cost more time or money to fix.

What can go wrong: the chair holds the issue instead of escalating it. "We'll handle it ourselves" becomes the answer, and by the time it does surface it has grown into a bigger problem, cost more to fix, or already affected the customer.

Tone

Good: problems are welcomed. Surfacing one early is rewarded, not punished.

What can go wrong: problems get punished. Whoever brings the bad news gets shot at, so the next person learns to stay quiet instead.

IMI bands used for the overall read

Dark, Named, Started, Compliant, Regulating, Embedded, Compounding. The overall score across all six dimensions places the meeting on this scale for that visit.
