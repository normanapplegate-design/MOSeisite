MOSei -- MOS Pressure Test
A stress-test diagnostic for consultants who need to know not just where a system stands today, but whether it will hold when pressure spikes. Scores baseline capacity, then models four common failure scenarios.
WHY

A Gap Score tells you where a system stands right now. It doesn't tell you what happens when demand spikes, a key supplier fails, staffing drops, or a critical system goes down. This tool answers that question directly.

Purpose

Score 12 dimensions of pressure-to-regulation capacity, then apply four pre-built stress scenarios to see how each dimension would likely perform under that specific pressure event.

Inputs

Observer scoring based on direct evidence, same standard as the Gap Scoring Tool. For best results, run both tools in the same engagement -- the Gap Score gives current state, the Pressure Test gives resilience.

Outputs

A baseline Pressure Capacity score, a 12-dimension breakdown, and a stress-test projection for each of four scenarios showing which dimensions are most likely to fail first under that specific pressure type.

Cadence

Run once per engagement as a structural assessment, or before/after a major operational change to verify resilience improved. Less frequent than the Gap Score -- this measures structural capacity, not week-to-week performance.

Escalation Path

Any dimension scoring below 4/10 against a likely scenario (demand spike, supplier failure, staffing loss, system outage) should be flagged as a single point of failure and escalated immediately, regardless of overall score.

Supporting Documents

Pairs with the Gap Scoring Tool for current-state diagnosis. The two together give a consultant both where the system stands and how it will hold under pressure.

Three Things Most Likely to Fail in Year One
1
Scoring resilience the system hasn't actually demonstrated. If you haven't observed the system under real pressure, score conservatively -- untested capacity isn't capacity.
2
Treating all four scenarios as equally likely. Weight your narrative toward the scenario most relevant to this client's actual risk profile, not a generic list.
3
Presenting the stress test without the baseline Gap Score. Resilience numbers mean little without current-state context. Always pair the two.

12 dimensions of pressure capacity

1
Capacity Flex -- can output scale up or down without breaking process discipline?
2
Decision Speed -- how quickly can this system make a high-stakes call under pressure?
3
Supplier Redundancy -- is there a viable backup if a key supplier fails?
4
Cross-Training Depth -- can roles be covered if key staff are unavailable?
