This is a self-report reflection tool built on the Norman System's operating logic. It is not a medical, psychological, or diagnostic instrument, and it does not replace a conversation with a doctor, therapist, or someone you trust. If something here is heavier than a tool should carry alone, that is worth taking to a person, not just a score.

WHY

Every MOSei tool so far measures the gap between external pressure and internal regulation at the level of a leader or a team. Norman's Gap does not stop being true just because the unit shrinks to one person. Someone's workload, finances, caregiving load, and health can compress them the same way an unregulated production line compresses under demand, and most people have never had a structured way to see it, only a vague sense that something is "off." Regulate makes the case for internal regulation as a discipline, including the nervous system itself: the ability to actually reset once you notice you're activated, not just manage the load intellectually. The Regulation App is where a reader stops reading about it and measures their own.

Purpose

Give an individual, not a manager, not an organization, a structured, field-aware read on where their external pressure sits relative to their internal regulation capacity, and which specific area is most likely to give first if nothing changes. The Regulation App is the individual-facing counterpart to the leadership tools: same Norman's Gap logic, one person instead of one org.

Inputs

  • Field or role selection (sets pressure weighting only)
  • Seven external pressure ratings: workload and role demands, financial pressure, caregiving and family load, health status, life transition, relational and social conflict, information and technology load
  • Eight internal regulation ratings: sleep, movement, reflective practice, resetting, social support, financial systems, boundaries, sense of purpose

Outputs

  • Gap Score, a single number from -100 to 100
  • Gap Index label: Regulated, Compressing, Strained, or Critical Gap
  • Pressure Index and Regulation Index shown side by side
  • A short narrative naming the highest-weighted pressure and the weakest regulation habit: the place most likely to give first
  • Two calls to action: a link to buy Regulate, and a separate link to MOSei.org for the full system

Cadence

Monthly is a reasonable default for someone tracking their own trend. Re-score immediately after a major life change: new job, new baby, a health event, a move, since those are exactly the moments field-level pressure defaults stop matching reality.

Escalation Path

A Critical Gap result is a prompt to act, not a diagnosis. The recommended path is: name the single highest pressure and single weakest regulation habit out loud to one trusted person, then choose one regulation habit to rebuild before adding anything new. If the gap involves health, safety, or a sense of being unable to cope, that conversation belongs with a professional, not with this tool.

Supporting Documents

Regulate is the primary companion text. This app is the practical instrument for the book's argument, not a standalone concept. Building a Management Operating System and The Norman System remain the leadership-facing references for readers who want to take the same logic back to their team.

Three Things Most Likely to Fail in Year One

  • Self-report bias on regulation habits: people rate what they intend to do, not what they actually do, which quietly deflates the gap
  • Field presets get treated as prescriptive instead of illustrative, so someone outside the seven listed fields feels the tool was not built for them
  • A one-time score with no cadence attached does not change behavior. Without a nudge to re-check, this becomes a novelty instead of a habit
