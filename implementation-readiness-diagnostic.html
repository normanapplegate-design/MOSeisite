Can You Actually Prove Your AI Is Working?
Most AI ROI calculators ask what the tool costs and guess at what it saves. This asks a harder question first: does your organization even have the structure in place to know? Four dimensions, one weakest link, no fake precision.

Works the same way whether you're evaluating an AI tool or rolling out any other software - switch the setting above to match.

What this tool measures

Adopting AI is external pressure - competitors, boards, vendors all pushing in the same direction. Whether that pressure produces real return depends on internal regulation: a stable baseline to measure against, a decision it actually feeds, a named owner, and a recurring review. Skip any one of those and the ROI question can’t be answered - not because the AI failed, but because nothing was in place to prove it either way.

This is Norman’s Gap applied to a single, specific pressure source. The output isn’t a dollar figure. It’s a readiness score and a named weakest dimension - the one thing blocking you from ever knowing if this is paying off.

The five readiness bands
Dark (0–1)No visibility. Any ROI claim right now is a guess.
Named (2–3)Aware of the gaps. Nothing measured yet.
Started (4–5)Partial visibility. At least one dimension still open.
Compliant (6–7)Most dimensions solid. ROI is mostly provable.
Regulating+ (8)Full visibility. ROI provable and compounding.
What this tool scores
1
Signal baseline - Did you have a stable, documented measure of this process before the AI arrived? No baseline, no honest before/after.
2
Decision window fit - Does the output feed a decision quickly, or just sit there? Task-time savings without decision-time savings rarely shows up as real return.
3
Ownership clarity - If the output was wrong for two weeks straight, would anyone named notice?
4
Failure condition exposure - Is there a scheduled review independent of whether something breaks, or does review only happen after the fact?

Based on Norman’s Gap, Norman Failure Condition, and the Norman Decision Window frameworks by Norman Applegate. Published by MOSei.org.
