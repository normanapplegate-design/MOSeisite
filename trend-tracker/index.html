Norman's Law Trend Tracker

A single Gap Index score is a data point. The trend across four to twelve weeks is the actual diagnosis — whether the intervention is working, or pressure is winning.

Why

Norman's Law states the condition plainly: when external pressure exceeds internal regulation, instability follows. Norman's Gap is that condition measured. A single reading tells you where things stand today.

It does not tell you whether things are getting better or worse. A Gap Index of 1.35 that has been falling for six weeks is a system correcting. A Gap Index of 1.35 that has been rising for six weeks is a system approaching System Risk. The number is identical. The prognosis is not.

Purpose

Log a Gap Index reading — External Pressure against Internal Regulation — for the same subject repeatedly over time. See the trend line, not just the point. Know whether an intervention is closing the gap before the outcome tells you.

Inputs
  • A subject to track — a plant, a team, or a single leader — scored the same way each time
  • External Pressure: consequence tier and how confirmed the pressure is right now
  • Internal Regulation: System Regulation across four factors, and Leader Regulation across three
  • Repeat readings, roughly weekly to monthly, for the same subject
Outputs
  • Gap Score and Gap Index for each reading, banded per the white paper's five states
  • A trend line across all readings logged for the subject
  • An automatic direction indicator — widening, stable, or closing — computed from the trend itself, not eyeballed
Cadence

Log a reading before starting any intervention, then again every one to four weeks after. Fewer than three readings will not show a real trend — treat early readings as a baseline, not a verdict.

Escalation Path

A widening trend goes to whoever owns the intervention, immediately — not at the next scheduled review. A closing trend is confirmation to keep doing what is already working.

Supporting Documents

The Norman System white paper, Section 5 (Calculating and Interpreting the Gap) and Section 7 (Longitudinal Application).

Three Things Most Likely to Fail in Year One
  • Scoring drifts between readings — a different observer, a different mood, a different day of the week. Score the same way, the same person if possible, every time.
  • One good reading gets treated as proof the intervention worked. Three or more readings in the same direction is a trend. One is a data point.
  • This tool's log lives in this browser only. Clearing browser data or switching devices will lose the history — export or print periodically if the record matters.
